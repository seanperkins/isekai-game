Shrines and upgrade places

How twelve games handle the place where you rest, spend, and come back after dying, drawn as eight verbs on one wheel, with what each one means for our altars.

How to read the wheels

Every place gets the same wheel of eight verbs, clockwise from the top. A filled dot means the place does it, a ringed dot means partly (or through a neighbouring object), and a faint dot means not.

  • Rest Heal or restore at the place.
  • Respawn Dying sends you back here.
  • Reset Resting brings the enemies back.
  • Power-up Gain strength here, paying with a currency or just the visit.
  • Bank Turn what you carry into something that survives death.
  • Choose A real decision is made here: a loadout, a trade-off, a version.
  • Travel The place is also a fast-travel stop.
  • Grow The place itself gets better as you invest in it.

On each card the coloured bar on a step matches the verb it uses. sourced marks how a place works, taken from the linked wiki or article pages. my read marks my interpretation of why it works and what it means for us: opinion, not fact.

Eight patterns that keep turning up

One landmark, several verbs

Rest, respawn and spending sit on a single object, so the player learns one landmark and returns to it by habit.

Seen in: Dark Souls, Blasphemous, Salt and Sanctuary

Ours: Ours already joins respawn, banking and a perk. Rest is the one verb left outside, in the Glow Pools.

Rest has a price

Healing brings the enemies back. That makes every shrine a decision, and the distance between shrines sets the tension.

Seen in: Dark Souls, Hollow Knight, Blasphemous, Salt and Sanctuary

Ours: Glow Pools heal without setting a respawn, and the altar sets a respawn without healing: the price is split in two. Worth deciding on purpose.

Spend here, or bank here and spend at home

Some games let you spend at the shrine; others convert the pile into something safe there and let you spend later at a hub.

Seen in: Spend at the shrine: Dark Souls, Salt and Sanctuary. Bank, then spend elsewhere: Dead Cells, Rogue Legacy 2, Darkest Dungeon, Hades.

Ours: We bank at the altar and spend the head start in her menu: the second pattern, and the cleaner of the two when the pile is lost at death.

One pick per visit

Offer two good things and allow one. The scarcity is what makes the choice mean something, and it needs no currency.

Seen in: Slay the Spire

Ours: Our altar menu allows everything every time. If more verbs arrive, this is the lever.

A checkpoint with a cost

Let the player place a checkpoint, but make it cost something scarce, then keep free fixed ones nearby so it never feels cruel.

Seen in: Ori and the Blind Forest

Ours: Probably not for us: it competes with the 'attune once' idea. Noted as the road not taken.

The place itself grows

Investing makes the landmark better, so progress is something you can see in the world rather than a number in a menu.

Seen in: Blasphemous, Dark Souls (kindling), Darkest Dungeon, Hades (the Mirror), Dead Cells (the forge)

Ours: The cheapest high-feedback idea in the guide: add rings or motes to the altar for each perk bought.

Each shrine has a name

Give each shrine its own faction, creed or offering, so where you rest changes what you can do.

Seen in: Salt and Sanctuary, Breath of the Wild (statues in different villages)

Ours: Already our plan: one altar per area, each selling its own perk. Look and sound per area would finish the idea.

Dying is a visit

Make the place you return to a destination in its own right: conversation, change, a reason to want to arrive.

Seen in: Hades, Rogue Legacy 2, Darkest Dungeon

Ours: Her menu is our version. Her lines react to what killed you; the pull grows with how much she remembers.

The twelve places

Dark Souls

The bonfire

Rest and checkpointUpgrade altars

Does: Rest · Respawn · Reset · Power-up · Grow

  1. Reach a bonfire
  2. Rest: health and Estus refill
  3. Every ordinary enemy returns
  4. Level up with souls
  5. Kindle it with Humanity
  6. Die: wake here

What it does sourced

  • Resting refills health, stamina, spells and Estus and clears most ailments. It respawns every ordinary enemy and resets traps; bosses stay down. You cannot rest with enemies nearby.
  • The same fire is where you spend souls to level up.
  • Kindling a bonfire with one Humanity gives it more Estus, up to three times.

Why it works my read

Resting costs you the ground you cleared, so every fire is a decision, and the gap between fires sets the tension. Rest, respawn and spending sit on one landmark, so there is only one thing to learn.

For our altar my read

Our altar already joins respawn (attune) to spending. The open question is whether resting should live here too; today it lives in the Glow Pools.

Hollow Knight

The bench, and the Stag Station

Rest and checkpoint

Does: Rest · Respawn · Reset · Choose · Travel (partly)

  1. Sit on a bench
  2. Heal; the map updates
  3. Swap your Charms
  4. Rooms reset
  5. Die: wake on this bench
  6. A Stag Station is a separate stop: pay Geo to travel

What it does sourced

  • Sitting on a bench heals you, lets you swap Charms, updates your map, respawns enemies and sets your respawn point.
  • Benches are the only place to swap Charms. They do not fast travel: Stag Stations do, and each must be unlocked with Geo.
  • The team took cues from the Souls games' checkpointing. Sparse benches in places like the White Palace are intentional; players argue over whether that is too harsh.

Why it works my read

Because Charms only change at benches, planning a route becomes planning around benches. The distance between benches is how the game spends your nerve.

For our altar my read

If we ever add loadout slots (the part-slot perk is the candidate), tying the change to altars is the cheap way to give them a second job.

Hades

The House and the Mirror of Night

Rebirth hubsUpgrade altars

Does: Respawn · Reset · Power-up · Choose · Grow · Bank (partly)

  1. Die
  2. Return to the House
  3. Talk: the story moves on
  4. Spend Darkness at the Mirror
  5. Pick the red or green version
  6. Leave for a new run

What it does sourced

  • Dying returns Zagreus to the House of Hades, the hub, where characters respond to where you are in the story.
  • Darkness gathered in runs is spent at the Mirror on permanent upgrades. Many are locked behind Chthonic Keys, and each ability has a red and a green version with only one active.
  • By mid-game Darkness runs out of things to buy, so the Dark Thirst buff extends to Gemstones, the renovation currency.

Why it works my read

The hub makes failure something you walk toward: the story advances when you die. A currency that runs dry is handed a second use instead of going dead.

For our altar my read

Her menu after death is our House moment. Her lines already react to what killed you; more conversation than numbers would carry the same pull.

Dead Cells

The Collector and the Legendary Forge

Upgrade altars

Does: Power-up · Bank · Choose · Grow (partly)

  1. Clear a biome
  2. Reach the passage
  3. Hand your cells to the Collector
  4. Pick what to invest in
  5. Progress on it is saved, even part paid
  6. Walk on, or break the door and keep the cells

What it does sourced

  • The Collector takes your accumulated cells to invest in upgrades, weapons and mutations at passages between biomes. A door stops you leaving until you have spent them, but it can be broken.
  • You can pay an upgrade in parts: whatever you invest is saved as progress.
  • After boss biomes the Legendary Forge raises the quality of gear found in later runs; each rank must be full before the next.

Why it works my read

Cells vanish when you die, so a spending stop that comes before every long stretch turns them into permanent progress while they are still safe. The breakable door lets people who want to hoard take the risk.

For our altar my read

The closest cousin to our banking: essence you hold is gone when you die, so you bank it at an altar. The Collector shows partial payments are fine.

Rogue Legacy 2

The Castle and the Docks

Rebirth hubsUpgrade altars

Does: Respawn · Power-up · Choose · Grow · Bank (partly)

  1. Die
  2. Back at the Castle: you keep all your gold
  3. Spend it on the Manor
  4. New classes and permanent stats
  5. Choose a class
  6. Go again

What it does sourced

  • Each time you die you return to the castle and keep all the gold you earned.
  • Gold buys Manor upgrades (more classes, permanent stats, new NPCs at the Docks) and upgrades from friends down at the docks.
  • Weapon and armor upgrades carry over after death.

Why it works my read

Nothing is lost at death except the run, so the only decision at home is what to buy. Spending becomes the reward for failing.

For our altar my read

Our soul points also survive death and buy things for the next life, so the shape matches. We split banking (the altar) from the head start (her menu).

Slay the Spire

The campfire

Rest and checkpointUpgrade altars

Does: Rest · Power-up · Choose

  1. Arrive at a rest site
  2. Pick one
  3. Rest: heal 30% of max HP
  4. or Smith: upgrade one card
  5. Move on

What it does sourced

  • A rest site offers Rest or Smith. Rest heals 30% of your max HP; Smith upgrades one card of your choice. You get one.
  • Guides to the sequel describe the same choice as one of the most important recurring decisions: a safe heal now against a stronger deck for every later fight.

Why it works my read

One action per visit makes every campfire a trade between safety now and strength later. No currency is involved, so there is nothing to track.

For our altar my read

If an altar ever offers several verbs at once, a one-pick-per-visit rule is the cheapest way to make the choice matter. Ours lets you do everything every time.

Blasphemous

The Prie Dieu

Rest and checkpointUpgrade altars

Does: Rest · Respawn · Reset · Grow · Travel (partly)

  1. Kneel at the altar
  2. Health and flasks refill
  3. Enemies in the area return
  4. The last altar is where you wake
  5. Donate Tears at the church
  6. The altars restore Fervour, then link up to teleport

What it does sourced

  • A Prie Dieu is an altar where the Penitent One kneels to rest: all health and flasks refill, enemies in the area respawn, and it becomes the respawn point.
  • In the first game, donating Tears of Atonement to the church upgrades every Prie Dieu: at 10,000 they restore Fervour, at 20,000 you can teleport between them.
  • In the sequel, altars are upgraded by finding and collecting Cobijadas.

Why it works my read

The altar is the thing that gets better. One donation improves every shrine in the world and turns plain checkpoints into a travel network.

For our altar my read

Show perks on the altar itself. It already glows; rings or motes added for each perk bought would give the same 'the place grew' payoff for almost nothing.

Zelda: Breath of the Wild

Goddess Statues and Shrines

Blessing shrinesUpgrade altars

Does: Power-up · Choose · Bank (partly)

  1. Find a Shrine
  2. Complete its trial
  3. Receive a Spirit Orb
  4. Offer four to a Goddess Statue
  5. Choose a Heart Container or a Stamina Vessel

What it does sourced

  • Spirit Orbs are given for completing a Shrine's trial: 120 in the base game.
  • At a Goddess Statue, found in villages and temples, four Spirit Orbs buy a Heart Container or a Stamina Vessel.
  • The Horned Statue in Hateno Village lets you change your mind later, for Rupees.

Why it works my read

Earning and spending happen in different places, so the blessing feels given. The respec statue removes the fear of choosing wrong.

For our altar my read

Our perks cannot be refunded. If perk choices ever branch, a refund statue like the Horned one is the standard pressure valve.

Ori and the Blind Forest

Soul Links and Spirit Wells

Rest and checkpoint

Does: Rest · Respawn · Choose

  1. Reach a safe moment
  2. Spend one Spirit Energy: a Soul Link
  3. It saves, and it is where you wake
  4. Spirit Wells are fixed and free
  5. A Well restores life and energy and saves

What it does sourced

  • Soul Links let you save anywhere you are safe, but each costs a point of Spirit Energy, which is not common.
  • Spirit Wells are fixed save points that fully restore life and energy and cost nothing.

Why it works my read

The checkpoint is a decision with a price, so skipping the save before a risky stretch is a visible gamble. The free Wells keep it from feeling punishing.

For our altar my read

A rare player-made respawn paid for with banked essence would be the Ori move, but it fights our 'attune once' idea. Probably not for us.

Metroid

Save rooms, recharge stations and teleportals

Rest and checkpoint

Does: Respawn · Rest (partly) · Travel (partly)

  1. Enter a save room
  2. Step on the platform
  3. Save
  4. Recharge is a separate room, or your ship
  5. In Dread, teleportals link in pairs

What it does sourced

  • In Super Metroid the save station is a whole room with a platform in the middle. You can also save in your ship, which restores your energy and ammo.
  • Energy recharge units are separate domed machines in their own blue rooms. Dread keeps them as Chozo statuary.
  • Dread's teleportals work in colour-coded pairs: use one and you arrive at its match.

Why it works my read

Giving the checkpoint a whole room makes it a landmark you navigate by and a calm beat after tension.

For our altar my read

A quiet room around each altar already follows this: our room lint keeps a clear zone around every altar.

Salt and Sanctuary

The Sanctuary

Rest and checkpointUpgrade altarsBlessing shrines

Does: Rest · Respawn · Reset · Power-up · Choose · Grow

  1. Arrive at a Sanctuary
  2. Claim it for your creed, or join its creed
  3. Make offerings
  4. Rest: refill, enemies return
  5. Spend salt on Black Pearls
  6. Die: wake here

What it does sourced

  • Sanctuaries are respawn points. Resting refills health, mana and creed items and resets every enemy except bosses.
  • You can claim a Sanctuary for your own creed or join the creed already there. You can only make offerings once aligned with its creed.
  • Salt is spent for Black Pearls to use in the skill tree. When you die, an enemy absorbs your salt and you must defeat it to get it back.

Why it works my read

A shrine that belongs to a faction gives each area an identity, and gives choosing a creed consequences wherever you rest.

For our altar my read

This is our 'each area has its own altar with its own perk' idea in another genre: what the sanctuary is changes what you can do there.

Darkest Dungeon

The Hamlet

Rebirth hubsUpgrade altars

Does: Power-up · Bank · Choose · Grow · Rest (partly)

  1. Return from an expedition
  2. Carry home heirlooms
  3. Spend them upgrading buildings
  4. Abbey, Sanitarium, Blacksmith
  5. Heroes recover and arm
  6. Send the next party

What it does sourced

  • Heirlooms (busts, portraits, deeds and crests) upgrade the buildings in the Hamlet. Each building wants the same kinds each time, and the amounts rise exponentially.
  • The Abbey relieves stress, the Sanitarium removes negative quirks and diseases, and the Blacksmith upgrades weapons and armor.
  • One analysis calls the Hamlet the meta-game that ties everything together; the same piece notes the mid-game can feel little different from the early game.

Why it works my read

One resource per building means the player always knows where an heirloom is worth taking. The town is the progress bar.

For our altar my read

We spend one currency in one menu. The Hamlet is the other path: several landmarks that each want their own resource. It pays off only with more content than we have.

What this suggests for our altars

Our altar: attune it once and it becomes a place you respawn; bank the essence you hold into soul points; buy its one local perk; the goddess's menu after death decides where, as whom and with what head start. These are suggestions, not decisions.

Where we sit on the grid: strong on respawn, banking and spending; nothing on rest, reset, travel or growth. The suggestions mostly fill in the second half.

  1. 1

    Show the perk on the altar.

    Add a ring or a few motes to each altar for every perk bought there. Blasphemous, Dark Souls' kindling and Darkest Dungeon's buildings all show that the place growing is the best feedback investing can get.

    Small, visual, no new system. Suggest doing it.

  2. 2

    Decide where rest lives, on purpose.

    Most games put rest, respawn and spending on one object. We split them: the altar respawns and spends, the Glow Pool rests. Ori and Metroid split save from heal too, so it is respectable, but the player should be told which object does what.

    A design decision for you; no change needed.

  3. 3

    Keep the bank-before-you-die tension.

    Essence you hold is lost on death, which is what makes banking feel urgent, as in Dead Cells. Resist adding a rule that protects held essence.

    Already how it works. Keep it.

  4. 4

    Let each area's altar look like its area.

    Salt and Sanctuary's creeds and the statues of Breath of the Wild give each shrine an identity. We have one perk per area; a look and a sound per area would finish it.

    Art and audio follow-up.

  5. 5

    Make the goddess the House.

    Hades keeps the player coming back by moving the story on death. Her lines already react to the cause of death; letting her remember (how many deaths, which altars) would carry the same pull.

    Copy and data, not code.

  6. 6

    If perks ever branch, add a refund.

    Breath of the Wild's Horned Statue lets you undo a choice for Rupees. Our perks are repeatable purchases with a rising price, so there is nothing to refund yet.

    Only if the perk design changes.

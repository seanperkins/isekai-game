1. Concept

Built

Doc direction

A side-scrolling Godot platformer where you die, reincarnate as monsters, and evolve each new body through what it eats and learns.

  • Inspirations: So I'm a Spider, So What? and That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime.
  • Structure from Dead Cells and Metroidvanias: runs with permanent unlocks that open new routes.
  • Current state: a slime is the first playable form.

In the game today

  • All of the above runs. Godot 4, 2D action platformer at 640×360. The slime is the only playable body: tackle to stun, hold to eat, gain essence, XP and a stat bonus.
  • Death resets the run. What you found persists: Compendium, Bestiary, map, opened shortcuts, read tablets, attuned altars, soul points, perks bought and the body forms you reached (scripts/persistence/profile.gd).
  • Scale: 23 rooms in 4 areas, 17 creature types, 32 skills, 24 body forms. 134 scripts (about 15.1k lines) and 186 test files with 1,887 test functions (counts only; the suite last passed at 1,851 tests when the essence overhaul and the skill tree were merged). A movement model with a sandbox for four species sits beside the game and is not wired into the player yet.

Decision needed

None: this section is aligned. One pointer: the doc now names Hollow Knight for the fixed map and Dead Cells for permanent unlocks, which settles the structure question in section 8.

2. Opening sequence

Doc only

Doc direction

  1. An unavoidable truck death. It plays the first time only: a joke for anyone who knows isekai, not a real introduction. Working idea: a JRPG-style turn-based fight where you try to dodge the trucks.
  2. A god or goddess apologizes: it was an accident.
  3. She offers reincarnation. At first the only option is slime.

She recurs: she remembers every previous life, reacts to how you died, and her menu grows over the game. She seems a helpful ally until the reveal: she is using you to harvest souls from this world, and eventually becomes the antagonist.

The voice that announces skills and evolutions might be the voice of the world.

In the game today

  • No truck and no goddess anywhere in the code or data (I searched).
  • A run starts directly in room C1 as a slime.
  • Death shows a card, "You dissolve. A new life begins.", for 1.5 seconds, then restarts (scripts/run.gd).
  • The only narrator is "A voice": the translucent pop-ups for skill unlocks and evolutions (code name Great Sage).
  • After death the goddess speaks a line for what killed you. Her menu (scripts/ui/goddess_menu.gd) opens when a second altar is attuned or there are soul points to spend: it lists places, unlocked species and a head start of levels and owned powers.

Decision needed

Working direction: the goddess and "A voice" are separate characters. The voice is the voice of the world (it already announces skills and evolutions), and she owns the death and rebirth moment, which already has a card and a menu to hang on. Still open: the truck scene's beats (which menu choices exist, how long it runs, what it introduces) and what teaches movement now that the dodging is turn-based. The game has no turn-based UI, so a scripted one-off is cheap and a real battle system is not.

3. Creature design

Partly built

Doc direction

Every monster is a potential reincarnation, defined by two axes: how it moves, and how it gains essence. Every enemy is a potential you.

  • Movement archetypes with shared controllers: bouncer (slime), crawler (spider), biped (goblins, skeletons; the fey is dropped for now). Later: quadruped. Burrower and flier are off the table for now.
  • Slime feel: the slime should feel like a slime from a fantasy game: squash and stretch, gooey physics and springiness, split by place. Tight on the ground, so walking and platforming stay precise; gooey in the air and on walls; springy landings.
  • Spider: web-shooting (catch or slow at range, lay ground traps), wall and ceiling walking, poison, devours what it traps. It is the next playable species, once the slime is in a good place: the essence overhaul, the skill tree screen, and slime movement that feels like a slime from a fantasy game.
  • Goblin: scavenges and crafts; power comes from inventory. Decided: two layers. Improvised throwables and traps (combine items mid-fight) are the active layer; one weapon slot and one armor slot, filled from loot, are the passive layer. Every goblin form can use items, and early on it throws rocks. It eats living things only to heal and learns the nature of things through combat, taming and interactions; a goblin tamer evolution is wanted. Decided: learning pays essence as lessons per creature type (the first defeat, taming or interaction with each type teaches its elements and pays that essence once; repeats pay a trickle); taming means offering a weakened creature a fitting item, and it follows and fights as a companion, one at a time, lost for good if it dies; a radial menu on a held modifier button holds items, combine and gear swap, with time slowed.
  • Undead: one branching tree: flesh, bone-ranged and bone-magic paths. Decided: undead drain life force from the living; a zombie or vampire devours flesh or blood instead; skeleton types graft parts onto their bodies (the parts you wear steer the path); vampires use items. The vampire is a new form, tied to blood, and decided to sit at the top of the flesh path (skeleton, zombie, ghoul, vampire). A graft gives a passive tied to the part and also nudges the skeleton toward a path.

In the game today

  • One playable species. A species id ("slime") is carried through the run and the save, but there is no species definition; the forms spec says to add one when a second species is designed.
  • Enemies are a separate roster: 17 creature types plus water pools. Their behavior comes from flags (charger, dropper, drifter, swimmer, pack, stomper), not shared player controllers (scripts/skills/creature_def.gd).
  • Spider ingredients already exist: Sticky Thread (now unlocked by eating three Black Spiders), Swing Thread, Binding Web, Wall Cling and Poison Breath (water and dark) as slime skills; Ceiling Walk as an enemy-only trait on the Black Spider, Vine Snake and Taratect. The slime's Weaver form line is the closest to a spider path.
  • Goblin and undead: nothing. There is no inventory or crafting and no bone or death essence.
  • A second playable body needs its own frame sheets, controller and form tree (hit shapes are traced from each frame).
  • Slime movement today: drawn poses for rise, fall, landing (a squash), spread, wall and tackle, and a squelching four-frame run. Every room is validated against today's jump reach (about a 60 px apex, steps of at most 54 px), so changing the air and wall physics means re-checking the 23 rooms and the tests that pin them.
  • Movement model (new, not wired in): a shared ground-and-air step with biped, slime, spider and wolf profiles, a squash spring and a verb runner (the slime's Tackle, Ooze and puddle slide) run in a movement sandbox (scenes/movement_sandbox.tscn). The player does not use it yet, so the slime still moves as described above.

Decided: the spider is next, once the slime is in a good place, which means three things: the essence overhaul, the skill tree screen, and slime movement that feels like a slime from a fantasy game. You did not list the story spine (goddess opening, shrine and soul loop, final fight) or more slime content (art for the 20 tinted forms, more rooms) as required first. Its thread, wall and poison pieces exist, ceiling-walk AI exists, and two spider sheets (Black Spider, Taratect) are drawn. Movement feel is now described: squash and stretch, gooey physics and springiness, tight on the ground and gooey in the air and on walls. The first spider ships with plain devour: its wrap verb needs corpses, so wrap arrives later with the items milestone (decided). Still open: how far the air and wall physics can change before the rooms need re-checking.

4. Essence-acquisition methods

Partly built

Doc direction

The method varies by race group so forms feel distinct on one shared system.

  • Slime: devours everything: creatures, stone creatures, flesh and items, and all of it gives essence. Some items are too strong to digest, or take time to digest. Spider: devours living things only, so it is limited in what it can get essence from, and it carries items in cocoons.
  • Goblin: eats living things only to heal; learns the nature of things through combat, taming and interactions; every form can use items. (Replaces the old scavenge line.)
  • Skeleton and undead: drain life force from living creatures; zombie and vampire devour flesh and blood instead; skeleton types graft; vampires use items.
  • Decided: future species may also commune or bond with a pack (reusing the goblin's taming) or steal essence from a living enemy without killing it. The wolf devours living things like the spider, with its pounce as the kill verb. The fey biped is dropped for now.
  • Items (new, decided): besides essence the world holds collectable materials (stones, moss, sticks) and monster parts (a scorpion's tail, dragon scales). One shared item set; each species handles it differently. The slime devours items too, and a part it holds sits inside the jelly and grants a passive tied to the part (a sting from a scorpion tail, armor from dragon scales) until you die; a body holds only a few parts in visible slots that grow with evolution, about two at stage 1 up to about five at stage 4 (working numbers; decided: slot counts are tuned per species). A held part digests into essence while it grants its passive, and a part too strong to digest stays as a lasting passive. The spider carries items in cocoons, a couple at a time, and a cocoon can also hold living prey to be eaten later. Every goblin form uses items. Skeleton types graft, and vampires use items.

In the game today

  • One method: devour. Tackle a creature to stun it, then hold to eat. Each essence unit it carries is absorbed, and you also gain HP, MP, XP and an eat bonus (the Black Spider gives +1 ATK per 3 eaten).
  • Water pools can be eaten without a stun.
  • There are no drops, loot, items or gear. Items are a new system: designed now, built after the spider and the essence overhaul (decided: the first spider ships with plain devour, and wrap arrives with the items milestone).

Decision needed

Decided: yes to items, one shared set handled differently per species. That makes the doc's old line, that only the goblin needs an inventory, out of date. Parts come from corpses (decided): a kill leaves a corpse you can harvest a part from; rare creatures and bosses leave a rare part on top. Decided: the old "eat or harvest, not both" rule is species-specific. The slime eats corpses and items too, so a corpse is essence now or a part for a slot; the spider and the draining undead take essence while the creature is alive, so their choice comes at the kill (consume it alive, or kill it and harvest); the goblin eats only to heal, so a corpse is just parts. Today a creature is eaten alive after a stun and leaves no corpse, which already matches the living-only eaters, so the new work is the corpse and the harvest. Decided: a kill has two outcomes, stun and eat alive as today (essence, no corpse), or kill plainly and leave a corpse to harvest or, for the slime, to devour. Remains: items stay where you fell; a tight play-time clock strips soft items like moss and flesh first (after about 2 minutes) and hard ones like stone and scales last (after about 8); only one pile exists, and dying again replaces it. Still open: the item list, exactly which items count as soft or hard, what each species' verb gives in practice, which parts the undead can graft (decided: a part's shape steers the path, so flesh goes toward zombie, a bow-arm toward archer, a skull or focus toward lich), what each tamed creature adds, and the slot numbers per species.

5. Essence types

Partly built

Doc direction

Essence is elemental, and essences are used to create powers. Working set of eight base elements: fire, water, earth, air, dark, light, mind/psychic, blood. Earth covers stone, defense and raw force. Poison and Physical are not on the list: poison is made from water and dark, and earth takes the place of physical (decided). Lightning, ice, poison and spore are not base essences: they come from combining the others (working recipes in section 6). The first pass uses the five elements existing creatures can carry: water, earth, air, light and dark. Fire, mind and blood come later, with new creatures.

An essence reads differently per species: dark makes a lich on a skeleton, an assassin on a goblin and a shadow-stalker on a spider.

In the game today

Five elements: water, earth, air, light and dark (scripts/core/essences.gd), which replaced the ten trait essences. Fire, mind and blood arrive with new creatures.

  • Each creature carries up to eight units across one to three elements, for example Black Spider: water 1, dark 1; Stone Drake: earth 4; Pale Moth: air 5, dark 3. The Cave Serpent carries none.
  • Essence feeds essence skills by absorbed count, and mixes are built: Poison Breath needs water 4 and dark 4, Spore Cloud air 8 and dark 4, Jolt light 4 and air 4. The single-element skills are Echolocation (air 6), Hydraulic Propulsion (water 8), Body Armor (earth 6), Hardened Shell (earth 14) and Tremor (earth 59). Sticky Thread unlocks by eating three Black Spiders, not by an essence.
  • Essence you hold also pays for evolving powers: held is eaten minus spent, and recipes read what was eaten, so spending never undoes an unlock.
  • docs/ledgers/essence-calibration.md records how unlock timing moved against the old trait numbers (Spore Cloud now unlocks in the Cave).

Light plus air (lightning) and water plus air (ice) both work on day one. 6. Levels, skills and evolution

Partly built

Doc direction

  • Level gates evolution; the essence you consumed decides which evolutions are on the menu.
  • Levels raise stats, stats modify skills, skills level up through use.
  • Each evolution sets its own rule on your essence mix (a threshold, a ratio, whatever fits).
  • At an evolution level the player sees every evolution their current essence makes possible and picks one.
  • Working idea: for evolutions within your species, the gate is the powers you have unlocked, since powers correspond to essence. A lineage is offered when you own one of its powers. The upgraded (evolved) form of the power then picks the form after it: the branch you took decides which child form is offered.
  • Examples: stone/defense gives a spiked slime; poison gives a toxic slime.
  • A power is an ability or a passive. It can unlock through an essence combination (working recipes: lightning is fire and air, or light and air; ice is water and air; poison is water and dark; spore is air and dark, maybe with earth) or through other requirements: eat a particular creature, dodge a number of times, drop to 1 HP a number of times. Most powers need a single essence. A mix is for special powers: a few base powers, more powerful skills, and the occasional interesting branch of a power.
  • Powers level up in the background from use. Essence is how powers evolve, and evolving spends it.
  • Evolving should be as frictionless as possible. Working idea: when you hit a threshold the game tells you, and you press a button to evolve a power.
  • Working idea: the skill screen shows the skill tree and its branches, based on what you have unlocked with this species before. The tree grows as you find it: only discovered nodes show, plus ??? stubs for the branches of powers you own, and a recipe's exact ingredients appear only once earned.

In the game today

Two separate evolution systems are built, and both read the powers you own.

  • Body forms (data/forms/): four stages, 24 forms. Level cap 10 per stage (270, 403, 540 and 673 XP); the level resets to 1 on evolving. Stage 1 to 2 offers every lineage whose power you have reached (each stage-2 form lists its opening powers), with Greater Slime added when fewer than two are open; there is no ranking and no cap. Stage 2 to 3 offers the two children of your form; stage 3 to 4 is one Sovereign.
  • Skill evolutions: four base skills each branch into two exclusive evolutions, permanent for the life (8 in all; see the appendix). Each base power carries a price in held essence that both of its branches share: Hydraulic Propulsion water 6, Poison Breath water 6 and dark 10, Spore Cloud air 6 and dark 12, Sticky Thread dark 14 (starting values, tuned by play). Evolution Points are gone.
  • Skills level through use or hidden triggers; stats are HP, MP, ATK, DEF, SPD. Essence unlocks skills, steers body forms through the powers it unlocks, and is spent to evolve powers.
  • The unlock engine combines several conditions (all must hold) and can require eating a specific creature, so an essence mix and "eat a spider" are data, and both are in use. It has no "dodge" event and no "down to 1 HP" threshold (the low-HP event fires at 30%). Existing examples of "other requirements": Leap (jump 40 times), Wall Cling (touch walls 15 times), Pain Resistance (recover from low HP twice), Sticky Thread (eat three Black Spiders).
  • Skill screen today: tabs are Skills, Tree, Compendium, Bestiary and Map, plus Form once the body can evolve. The Tree tab is a zoomable graph of powers, their evolutions and the body forms. It grows as you discover things: ??? stubs one step past what you know, secrets absent until owned once. It is read-only, and moves by keys, D-pad, left stick or mouse click, with Z or L3 flipping the zoom. The Settings menu (Tab or R3) holds the sound sliders. The Compendium remembers across lives how far you have discovered each skill; it is one global store and not per species, and the body forms you reached are now saved too.
  • Levelling and evolving today: skill levels apply automatically and a ticker says "Your skills grew". Skill evolutions are the exception: a pop-up says "Evolution available", and once you hold the price a HUD line says "Evolve: <power>". Then you open the menu, pick the Skills tab, select the row, read its price (or what is short) and press twice to confirm. On a controller every button is taken in play: A jump, X tackle, B eat, Y inspect, the shoulder buttons and triggers are the four skill slots, the sticks and D-pad move and aim, L3 flips the tree zoom and R3 opens settings.
  • Only 4 of the 24 forms have their own art (the Weaver line); the other 20 use the tinted slime sprite.

Decision needed

Powers. Most of your list already fits the engine. New code is needed for "dodge X times" (no dodge event exists, and "dodge" needs a definition) and "down to 1 HP". Alternative recipes, such as lightning from fire and air or light and air, also need a small engine change, because a power has one list of conditions that must all hold. Open: do the two lightning recipes give the same power, or two flavors of it?

Direction: levels from use, evolution with essence. Powers keep levelling in the background from use, as they do today. Evolving a power spends essence (decided). That makes essence something you hold and can spend, so two numbers help: eaten (the running total this life, which recipes read, so spending never undoes an unlock) and held (what you can spend on evolutions and banking). If body-form offers read the powers you have unlocked (see Evolution below), spending never shrinks the form menu, and held only matters for evolving powers and banking. If essence pays for evolutions, EP has no job left. Decided and built: EP is gone (levels still raise stats and gate the body evolution at the cap).

Friction. Levelling needs no button. Evolving is the one moment that does, because it is a real choice (which branch). My suggestion is to make it one press with no menu trip: a persistent glow on the power's HUD slot (the pop-up is gone after 2.5 seconds), then a one-press choice. A short hold can replace today's two-press confirm, since an evolution is permanent for the life. A controller has no free button, so the prompt likely borrows an existing one while it is showing, as "Y: attune" does at altars. Decided: not every branch has a recipe. Most branches are open at the parent's evolve level; an interesting branch can need a mix of essences, so you see it only if you hold the right mix. Decided: each power has its own price in the element or elements it runs on, shared by its evolution branches (a mix power costs both), shown on the HUD prompt as ready once you hold enough. Open: the actual amounts.

Skill tree view. The power trees can be drawn from data the game already has: an evolution names its parent, and each base power has two branches. "Owned once" in the Compendium marks the branch you took in a past life. Decided: the tree grows as you find it. Only discovered nodes show, plus ??? stubs for the branches of powers you own, and a recipe's exact ingredients appear only once earned, which is the Compendium's rule today. What is missing: the knowledge is one global store, so each species needs its own, and a skill both species share, such as Sticky Thread, needs a rule for how it shows in the second species' tree (suggestion: "named" until that species earns it). A form tree also needs new persistence, because the form you reached is not saved. Decided: one combined, zoomable graph. Powers and evolutions are connected nodes in a single tree that zooms in and out. If evolutions are hard to show alongside powers, fall back to one evolution at a time, plus where the next evolution is once it is unlocked. New work: a node layout that stays readable at 640×360, pan and zoom controls (menus have free buttons, unlike play), and the saved form. Open: the controls, and the shared-skill rule above.

Evolution. Two kinds are built (body forms and skill evolutions), and your notes now cover both: skill evolutions spend essence, and body-form evolutions within your species are gated by the powers you have unlocked (a working idea, since powers correspond to essence). Stage 1 to 2 is built this way: before the overhaul one global rule (affinity of at least 0.6) decided it. Your "toxic slime" is the existing Toxic form; "spiked slime" has no match, and Bulwark is the closest. Built: a lineage is offered once you own one of its powers: Sticky Thread for Weaver, Hydraulic Propulsion for Tide, Poison Breath or Spore Cloud for Toxic, Body Armor, Hardened Shell or Tremor for Bulwark, Echolocation for Echo. Decided: the evolved power then picks the stage-3 form, so the branch you took decides which child is offered (for example Binding Web to Snare and Swing Thread to Arachne; the pairings still need authoring). Only four base powers have evolutions today (Sticky Thread, Hydraulic Propulsion, Poison Breath, Spore Cloud), so Bulwark and Echo have no branch to pick a child from and would keep offering both children. Toxic has four evolved powers for two children, so its pairing needs a rule. Powers last for the life, so spending essence never shrinks the form menu, and the skill tree can show which forms a power leads to.

7. Reincarnation and soul progression

Partly built

Doc direction

  • What you can reincarnate as reflects what your soul can become, and grows across runs.
  • Each species has its own unlock condition before it appears on the goddess's menu. The default is to eat or defeat N of the creature, with N scaled by rarity (about 10 common, 5 uncommon, 1 to 3 rare).
  • Soul points are earned on runs, mostly by banking essence at an altar at a flat rate whatever the element, and kept. They buy new species and four permanent perks: an extra part slot, slower remains decay, a bigger death seed and stronger base stats, each bought several times at a rising price.
  • A stronger soul can start you further along a species' evolution.
  • Decided: altars bank essence into soul points. One altar per area (Cave, Grotto, Flooded, Deep), the same menu at each, plus one fixed local perk: Cave base stats, Grotto the part slot, Flooded slower remains decay, Deep the bigger death seed.

In the game today

  • Altars in four rooms: C1 (default), G1, F1 and D1. Inspect one to open its menu: attune it (a respawn place in every later run), bank held essence into soul points, and buy its local perk.
  • Only the Cave altar sells a perk so far: stronger base stats (+2 max HP and +1 max MP a purchase, from the next life, 5 soul points and rising by 3). The other three altars have none yet. There are no free kits: the goddess's menu sells a head start (starting levels and Compendium-owned powers) for soul points.
  • The stage is always 1 on rebirth, and a head start banks no XP.
  • Persisted: Compendium, Bestiary, map, shortcuts, tablets, attuned altars, soul points and perks bought, last choice, species id and the body forms reached. Not persisted: essence counters, which reset each life.
  • Soul points, the perk purchase at an altar, the species list and species unlocks are built; the opening scene is not.
  • Useful plumbing: WorldProgress and SoulProgress save their sections through the Profile.

Decision needed

Decided: both, with one currency, and the pools are replaced by altars. The goddess's altars replace the rebirth pools: at an altar you attune it (it becomes a place you respawn), bank essence into soul points, and meet the goddess, who offers your next body and head start. The altar you choose decides "where" you start; soul points, spent at the altar, buy "how far along" (a starting level, a power); there are no free kits. The pools are built today and stay until the altar milestone (their seeded affinity is already gone); altars are decided: one per area (Cave, Grotto, Flooded, Deep), the same menu at each (unlocked species and the head start) plus one fixed local perk, and banking is a flat rate whatever the element. Perks, decided: an extra part slot, slower remains decay, a bigger death seed and stronger base stats, each bought several times at a rising price. Species unlocks, decided: the default rule is to eat or defeat N of that creature (the Bestiary and Compendium already count both), with N scaled by rarity; a special species swaps in its own condition, either defeating a rare creature or boss (the Taratect, the Cave Serpent) or eating it while in a given form. Still open: the soul-point prices and banking rate (tuned as data), the exact N per creature, which species take a special condition, and whether a soul can start a life past stage 1 of the evolution tree (the game always starts at stage 1).

8. World structure and gates

Partly built

Doc direction

  • One fixed, hand-built world, the same every life, with gates between sections. Death is a new life in the same world; no procedural generation. Decided.
  • Species act as runes: unlocking a form opens routes that were closed.
  • Gates are form-locked or skill-locked, and everything is solvable eventually.
  • Cross-run world state: one form changes the world for a later one (the bird opens the way for the wolf).

In the game today

  • One fixed hand-built map, the same every run. Procedural rooms were ruled out of scope.
  • 23 rooms in four areas: Cave 6, Grotto 5, Flooded Tunnels 6, Deep 6, on a shared grid of 640×360 screens. Rooms are authored in the in-game room editor and saved as data/rooms/*.tres; there is no generator (docs/rooms.md).
  • Gates: exits carry a wall_cling or swim label, but the label does not block the player. The block is the geometry (a chimney only wall cling climbs, a water shaft only a swimmer climbs). Three rare rooms sit behind gates: G5, F6 and D6.
  • No form-locked gates; Swim is a skill. A world validator and room lint check reachability and exit matching on every room.
  • Cross-run state that works today: shortcut switches (C6 opens a floor to C1 for good), read tablets, visited rooms.

Decision needed

Decided: keep the map fixed. Death is a new life in the same hand-built world, and permanent unlocks (shortcuts, species, soul points) open routes. The room editor, validator, grid and room-id save keys stay as they are. Still open: form-locked gates (today only geometry blocks, and Swim is a skill), and how a species unlock opens a route.

9. Ending a run, shrines and death

Partly built

Doc direction

  • Working idea: a run ends when you die, or when you beat the god of reincarnation. The god of reincarnation is the goddess (decided).
  • Shrines don't end a run; they bank essence into soul points.
  • Essence seed: when you die, you lose the essence you hold, but what killed you leaves a small seed of its element and your next life starts with it. Banking at a shrine, or spending on an evolution, is how you keep essence.
  • Open: whether a run can end any other way, and what else shrines do.
  • Story: the goddess seems a helpful ally until the reveal that she is using you to harvest souls from this world; she eventually becomes the antagonist.

In the game today

  • Runs end only by death. There is no win condition.
  • The Cave Serpent has a creature definition (40 HP) but is placed in no room, and no lair or victory screen exists. The last built room is D5.
  • Rest and save features: Glow Pools (rest only, not a checkpoint) in C4, G4, F5 and D5; tablets (lore plus a Compendium hint); altars (attune, bank essence, buy a perk). Only altars bank anything.
  • The death cause is not recorded for the player: Player.receive_hit takes a cause argument and ignores it, and died carries nothing. Enemies already tag kill causes for their death animations.

Decision needed

Three small calls, with my suggestions:

  • The final fight. Decided: the god of reincarnation is the goddess. The Cave Serpent, which the game planned as its goal, becomes an area boss on the way to her.
  • Shrines. Decided (with section 7): the goddess's altars replace the rebirth pools; one object attunes, banks essence and meets the goddess. Glow Pools (plain rest) are a separate object and are not decided here.
  • Essence seed. Decided: any elemental death leaves a small fixed seed: a creature kill seeds that creature's strongest element, a hazard seeds its own (poison water, drowning), and a fall or crush seeds nothing. The amount does not scale with what you held. Needs the death cause recorded on the player (today receive_hit ignores it) and a cause-to-element map for hazards. Open: the exact amount, and whether the seed carries across species.
  • The reveal. Decided: every soul point you bank feeds her, which makes the shrine the story's central choice (spend essence on powers now, or bank it for permanent progress), and she is the god of reincarnation you beat to end a run. Decided: the reveal comes late, before the last area, and after it banking stops: altars no longer bank essence, perks are bought with essence directly and a way to confront her opens. You keep the soul points you hold and can still spend them; her fight is fixed, not scaled by what you banked. Decided: a last area past the Deep opens with the reveal and ends in her fight. Open: what the last area holds.

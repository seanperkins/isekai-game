1. Concept

Built

Doc direction

A side-scrolling Godot platformer where you die, reincarnate as monsters, and evolve each new body through what it eats and learns.

  • Inspirations: So I'm a Spider, So What? and That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime.
  • Structure from Dead Cells and Metroidvanias: runs with permanent unlocks that open new routes.
  • Current state: a slime is the first playable form.

In the game today

  • All of the above runs. Godot 4, 2D action platformer at 640×360. The slime is the only playable body: tackle to stun, hold to eat, gain essence, XP and a stat bonus.
  • Death resets the run. What you found persists: Compendium, Bestiary, map, opened shortcuts, read tablets and attuned rebirth pools (scripts/persistence/profile.gd).
  • Scale: 23 rooms in 4 areas, 17 creature types, 32 skills, 24 body forms. 122 scripts (about 13.6k lines) and 170 test files with 1,705 test functions (counts only; I did not run the suite).

Decision needed

None: this section is aligned. One pointer: the doc cites Dead Cells for structure, while the game's map is fixed and Hollow Knight-style. That is the conflict in section 8.

2. Opening sequence

Doc only

Doc direction

  1. An unavoidable truck death. It plays the first time only: a joke for anyone who knows isekai, not a real introduction. Working idea: a JRPG-style turn-based fight where you try to dodge the trucks.
  2. A god or goddess apologizes: it was an accident.
  3. She offers reincarnation. At first the only option is slime.

She recurs: she remembers every previous life, reacts to how you died, and her menu grows over the game. She seems a helpful ally until the reveal: she is using you to harvest souls from this world, and eventually becomes the antagonist.

The voice that announces skills and evolutions might be the voice of the world.

In the game today

  • No truck and no goddess anywhere in the code or data (I searched).
  • A run starts directly in room C1 as a slime.
  • Death shows a card, "You dissolve. A new life begins.", for 1.5 seconds, then restarts (scripts/run.gd).
  • The only narrator is "A voice": the translucent pop-ups for skill unlocks and evolutions (code name Great Sage).
  • After death a rebirth menu appears only when two or more rebirth pools are attuned. It lists places, not species (scripts/ui/reincarnation_menu.gd).

Decision needed

Working direction: the goddess and "A voice" are separate characters. The voice is the voice of the world (it already announces skills and evolutions), and she owns the death and rebirth moment, which already has a card and a menu to hang on. Still open: the truck scene's beats (which menu choices exist, how long it runs, what it introduces) and what teaches movement now that the dodging is turn-based. The game has no turn-based UI, so a scripted one-off is cheap and a real battle system is not.

3. Creature design

Partly built

Doc direction

Every monster is a potential reincarnation, defined by two axes: how it moves, and how it gains essence. Every enemy is a potential you.

  • Movement archetypes with shared controllers: bouncer (slime), crawler (spider), biped (goblins, skeletons, maybe fey). Later: quadruped. Burrower and flier are off the table for now.
  • Spider: web-shooting (catch or slow at range, lay ground traps), wall and ceiling walking, poison, devours what it traps. It is the next playable species, once the slime is in a good place.
  • Goblin: scavenges and crafts; power comes from inventory.
  • Undead: one branching tree: flesh, bone-ranged and bone-magic paths.

In the game today

  • One playable species. A species id ("slime") is carried through the run and the save, but there is no species definition; the forms spec says to add one when a second species is designed.
  • Enemies are a separate roster: 17 creature types plus water pools. Their behavior comes from flags (charger, dropper, drifter, swimmer, pack, stomper), not shared player controllers (scripts/skills/creature_def.gd).
  • Spider ingredients already exist: Sticky Thread, Swing Thread, Binding Web, Wall Cling and poison as slime skills; Ceiling Walk as an enemy-only trait on the Black Spider, Vine Snake and Taratect. The slime's Weaver form line is the closest to a spider path.
  • Goblin and undead: nothing. There is no inventory or crafting and no bone or death essence.
  • A second playable body needs its own frame sheets, controller and form tree (hit shapes are traced from each frame).

Today 20 of 24 forms use the tinted slime art, there is no final fight, no shrine or soul loop and no goddess, and essences still use the old trait names. Pick which of these must be done before the spider starts.

4. Essence-acquisition methods

Partly built

Doc direction

The method varies by race group so forms feel distinct on one shared system.

  • Slime: devour. Spider: devour (eats what it traps).
  • Goblin: scavenge, looting corpses for gear and essence.
  • Skeleton and undead: not decided.
  • Other ideas: drain or siphon (parasites, undead), commune or bond (pack beasts).

In the game today

  • One method: devour. Tackle a creature to stun it, then hold to eat. Each essence unit it carries is absorbed, and you also gain HP, MP, XP and an eat bonus (the Black Spider gives +1 ATK per 3 eaten).
  • Water pools can be eaten without a stun.
  • There are no drops, loot or gear, so scavenge has nothing to attach to.

Decision needed

Do you want an inventory system at all? Goblin scavenge and craft is the only idea in the doc that needs one; every other species idea reuses what exists. If not, goblin could still be devour with a different movement and skill set.

5. Essence types

Conflicts

Doc direction

Essence is elemental, and essences are used to create powers. Working set of ten: fire, water, earth, air, poison, dark, light, physical/force, mind/psychic, blood. Lightning and ice are not base essences: they are powers made by combining essences (working recipes in section 6).

An essence reads differently per species: dark makes a lich on a skeleton, an assassin on a goblin and a shadow-stalker on a spider.

In the game today

Ten essences, but they are traits tied to specific creatures: sound, flight, poison, water, armor, earth, thread, spore, shell, shock (scripts/core/essences.gd).

  • Each creature carries one to three units, for example Black Spider: poison 1, thread 1; Stone Drake: earth 3, armor 1.
  • Essence feeds essence skills by absorbed count (Poison Breath: poison ×4; Tremor: earth ×24) and body-form lineage affinity.
  • flight is reserved and feeds no skill.

6. Levels, skills and evolution

Partly built

Doc direction

  • Level gates evolution; the essence you consumed decides which evolutions are on the menu.
  • Levels raise stats, stats modify skills, skills level up through use.
  • Each evolution sets its own rule on your essence mix (a threshold, a ratio, whatever fits).
  • At an evolution level the player sees every evolution their current essence makes possible and picks one.
  • Examples: stone/defense gives a spiked slime; poison gives a toxic slime.
  • A power is an ability or a passive. It can unlock through an essence combination (working recipes: lightning is fire and air, or light and air; ice is water and air) or through other requirements: eat a particular creature, dodge a number of times, drop to 1 HP a number of times.
  • Working idea: essences are also how powers are upgraded and eventually evolved.

In the game today

Two separate evolution systems are built.

  • Body forms (data/forms/): four stages, 24 forms. Level cap 10 per stage (270, 403, 540 and 673 XP); the level resets to 1 on evolving. Stage 1 to 2 offers up to three lineages whose affinity (essence eaten divided by the lineage's supply) is at least 0.6, with Greater Slime as a fallback. Stage 2 to 3 offers the two children of your form; stage 3 to 4 is one Sovereign.
  • Skill evolutions: four base skills each branch into two exclusive evolutions for 1 EP, permanent for the life (8 in all; see the appendix).
  • Skills level through use or hidden triggers; stats are HP, MP, ATK, DEF, SPD. Essence does not upgrade anything today: it unlocks skills and steers body forms.
  • The unlock engine already combines several conditions (all must hold) and can require eating a specific creature, so an essence mix and "eat a spider" are expressible as data. It has no "dodge" event and no "down to 1 HP" threshold (the low-HP event fires at 30%). Existing examples of "other requirements": Leap (jump 40 times), Wall Cling (touch walls 15 times), Pain Resistance (recover from low HP twice).
  • Only 4 of the 24 forms have their own art (the Weaver line); the other 20 use the tinted slime sprite.

Decision needed

Powers. Most of your list already fits the engine. New code is needed for "dodge X times" (no dodge event exists, and "dodge" needs a definition) and "down to 1 HP". Alternative recipes, such as lightning from fire and air or light and air, also need a small engine change, because a power has one list of conditions that must all hold. Open: do the two lightning recipes give the same power, or two flavors of it?

Essence as upgrade fuel. This needs a rule for what essence is. Today it is a running total that unlocks things and never goes down. If essence also upgrades powers and shrines bank it into soul points, it has to be a stock you can spend. My suggestion is two numbers: eaten (the running total this life, which unlocks read, so spending can never undo a recipe) and held (the stock you spend on upgrades, evolutions and banking). A second rule keeps the growth systems apart: how a power unlocked decides how it grows. Powers unlocked by doing (jumping, wall touches) keep growing by doing; powers unlocked by essence grow by essence. If essence also pays for skill evolutions, EP becomes unnecessary, which is a bigger change because EP is built.

Evolution. Does the doc's "evolution" mean body forms, skill evolutions, or both? The doc's per-evolution rule generalizes what the game already does: today there is one global rule (affinity at least 0.6) and it applies only at stage 1 to 2. Your "toxic slime" is the existing Toxic form; "spiked slime" has no match, and Bulwark is the closest. My suggestion: let each form carry an optional unlock condition that defaults to today's behavior, and show every qualifying form instead of capping at three.

7. Reincarnation and soul progression

Partly built

Doc direction

  • What you can reincarnate as reflects what your soul can become, and grows across runs.
  • Each species has its own unlock condition before it appears on the goddess's menu.
  • Soul points are earned on runs and kept. They buy new species and permanent perks.
  • A stronger soul can start you further along a species' evolution.
  • Working idea: shrines bank essence into soul points.

In the game today

  • Rebirth pools in four rooms: C1 (default), G1, F1 and D1. Attune one with Inspect and it is a respawn place in every later run.
  • Each non-default pool has a kit: granted skills, a starting character level and seeded affinity. D1 grants Leap, Wall Cling, Swim and Tremor at level 5, plus seeded thread 7 and water 6.
  • The stage is always 1 on rebirth, and a kit gives no EP.
  • Persisted: Compendium, Bestiary, map, shortcuts, tablets, attuned pools, last choice, species id. Not persisted: essence counters, which reset each life.
  • There is no currency, perk shop, species list or species unlock.
  • Useful plumbing: Progression.seeded already carries essence into the next life, but it only counts toward form affinity.

Decision needed

Where does the head start come from: the place you are reborn at (pools, today), the soul (points, your doc), or both? My suggestion is both with one currency: pools stay as "where", and soul points buy "how far along" at the pool you choose (a higher kit level or an extra kit skill). Note the clash: your doc lets a stronger soul start further along evolution, while the game always starts a new life at stage 1.

8. World structure and gates

Conflicts

Doc direction

  • Worlds are procedurally generated, with hand-made preset gates between sections.
  • Species act as runes: unlocking a form opens routes that were closed.
  • Gates are form-locked or skill-locked, and everything is solvable eventually.
  • Cross-run world state: one form changes the world for a later one (the bird opens the way for the wolf).

In the game today

  • One fixed hand-built map, the same every run. Procedural rooms were ruled out of scope.
  • 23 rooms in four areas: Cave 6, Grotto 5, Flooded Tunnels 6, Deep 6, on a shared grid of 640×360 screens. Rooms are authored in the in-game room editor and saved as data/rooms/*.tres; there is no generator (docs/rooms.md).
  • Gates: exits carry a wall_cling or swim label, but the label does not block the player. The block is the geometry (a chimney only wall cling climbs, a water shaft only a swimmer climbs). Three rare rooms sit behind gates: G5, F6 and D6.
  • No form-locked gates; Swim is a skill. A world validator and room lint check reachability and exit matching on every room.
  • Cross-run state that works today: shortcut switches (C6 opens a floor to C1 for good), read tablets, visited rooms.

Decision needed

Keep the map fixed, go procedural, or mix? My suggestion is a hybrid: keep the hand-built hub and gate skeleton, and assemble the stretches between gates procedurally from room pieces. The room editor already authors the "preset" pieces your doc describes, and the validator can check whatever gets assembled. It is still a large change, because rooms sit on fixed grid cells today and the map and the save are keyed by room id.

9. Ending a run, shrines and death

Conflicts

Doc direction

  • Working idea: a run ends when you die, or when you beat the god of reincarnation.
  • Shrines don't end a run; they bank essence into soul points.
  • Essence seed: when you die, what killed you leaves a small seed of its element and your next life starts with it.
  • Open: whether a run can end any other way, and what else shrines do.
  • Story: the goddess seems a helpful ally until the reveal that she is using you to harvest souls from this world; she eventually becomes the antagonist.

In the game today

  • Runs end only by death. There is no win condition.
  • The Cave Serpent has a creature definition (40 HP) but is placed in no room, and no lair or victory screen exists. The last built room is D5.
  • Rest and save features: Glow Pools (rest only, not a checkpoint) in C4, G4, F5 and D5; tablets (lore plus a Compendium hint); rebirth pools (attune). None of them banks anything.
  • The death cause is not recorded for the player: Player.receive_hit takes a cause argument and ignores it, and died carries nothing. Enemies already tag kill causes for their death animations.

Decision needed

Three small calls, with my suggestions:

  • The final fight. The game planned the Cave Serpent as the goal; your doc says the god of reincarnation. The serpent could become an area boss on the way to the god.
  • Shrines. Reuse rebirth pools: attune plus bank, so one object does both, and Glow Pools stay as plain rest.
  • Essence seed. Record which creature killed the player and seed that creature's own essence. The data already exists on every creature, so no new tagging scheme is needed.
  • The reveal. Soul points are the natural thing for her to harvest: every point you bank feeds her, which makes the shrine the story's central choice (spend essence on powers now, or bank it for permanent progress). Open: when the reveal happens, whether banking changes after it, and whether she is the god of reincarnation you beat to end a run.
